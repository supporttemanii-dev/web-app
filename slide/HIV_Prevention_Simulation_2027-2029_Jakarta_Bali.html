Method

A microsimulation with an agent-based network: 5,000 individual agents, simulated month by month

Microsimulation. Each agent is an individual synthetic person with their own age, identity, income, number of partners, sexual role, condom use, online partner seeking, PrEP history, lenacapavir preference and willingness to pay, drawn from the 2025–2026 network surveys in Jakarta and Bali (aggregate distributions only; no respondent record is used).

Agent-based network. Agents form and end main, casual and one-off partnerships, and some have partners outside the city. HIV passes act by act within these partnerships. Outreach workers link agents to testing and PrEP, and diagnosed agents refer their partners for testing.

Every scenario is run several times with different random seeds; charts show medians and 5th–95th percentile ranges. Results are reported per 10,000 MSM and transgender women and, if you enter population size estimates, as absolute numbers.

Agent-based model equations (monthly step, Jan 2026 – Dec 2032) & legend
Per act: p = β · fsrc · [π·prec + (1 − π)·pins] · (1 − c̄·εc) · (1 − εPrEP)
Per partnership-month: P(infection) = 1 − (1 − p)nacts; nacts = 8 main, 3 casual, 1 one-off
π = rj(1 − ri) ÷ [rj(1 − ri) + ri(1 − rj)] (probability the susceptible partner j is receptive)
fsrc = 3 (acute, first 3 months), 1 (undiagnosed), wD = 0.8 (diagnosed), 0 (suppressed)
Partnerships: main and casual formed by matching open "stubs"; dissolve with probability 1/18 and 1/4 per month; one-off contacts ~ Poisson(λi)
Testing: Pi(diagnosis) = 1 − (1 − ρ·Ti)1/12; Ti = Tfac + (T − Tfac)/coverage if linked to an outreach worker
Network testing: each month a diagnosed, outreach-linked agent refers each current partner with probability κ·sSNS·Y/2.9
PrEP start: Pi = e(t) · wi/w̄ · (1.6 if outreach-linked, else 0.4); wi = e0.5 zi
Oral PrEP: continue at month 3 with probability r3, then stop with δ per month; LEN: continue at each 6-monthly injection with rinj
Turnover: agents exit at μ = 1/180 per month (plus 0.002 if unsuppressed) and are replaced by new 18–25-year-olds
Calibration: β and ρ chosen per city so 2026 incidence ≈ 3.5/100 PY and status awareness stays ≈ 67% under current support

Appendix

Parameters, sources and limitations

ParameterValueSource

Reading the sources

Survey Jakarta 2026 / Bali 2025 network surveys. Programme data national programme data and planning targets. Assumption literature-informed values to be refined.

Limitations

  • Partnerships are formed by random matching of open "stubs" with simple age mixing, a simplified form of the network models used in EpiModel; partnership durations and act rates come from the literature.
  • Each run simulates 5,000 agents; results are scaled per 10,000 and reported as medians of a few replicate runs, so small differences (under about 20 infections) are within simulation noise.
  • Survey respondents were recruited through snowball and service channels, so survey levels of PrEP use and HIV prevalence are not used directly.
  • Targeting curves are derived from models predicting prevalent HIV, used as a proxy for future risk.
  • Lenacapavir uptake uses stated preferences, which typically overestimate actual uptake.
  • Effects of caseload, legal shocks and absorption are assumption-driven and should be read as sensitivity analyses. Calibration targets only 2026 incidence and status awareness.
  • Ranges reflect stochastic variation between runs, not parameter or structural uncertainty.
  • No costs are modelled; cost per infection averted requires unit costs from costed programme and transition plans.

The plan works on paper. Retention, the safety net and the transition decide whether it works in people.

About this model. A microsimulation with an agent-based network: 5,000 individual agents per run, each with traits drawn from the 2025–2026 network surveys in Jakarta and Bali, forming main, casual, one-off and external partnerships through which HIV passes act by act. Outreach workers link agents to testing and PrEP, and diagnosed agents refer partners.

Calibration and cross-check. Transmission and testing are calibrated to 2026 incidence and status awareness for each city; results are cross-checked against a deterministic compartmental model (appendix). Each scenario is run with several random seeds, and results are shown as medians and ranges.

All projections are illustrative and depend on the assumptions shown; they support, not replace, Spectrum/AEM and costed planning.
